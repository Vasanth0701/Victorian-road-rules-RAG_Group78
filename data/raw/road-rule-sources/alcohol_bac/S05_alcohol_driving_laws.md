# Alcohol and driving laws

Source ID: S05
Organisation: Transport Victoria
Source title: Alcohol and driving laws
URL: https://transport.vic.gov.au/road-and-active-transport/road-rules-and-safety/alcohol-drugs-and-driving/alcohol-and-driving-laws

## Blood alcohol concentration

Blood alcohol concentration (BAC) measures the amount of alcohol in a person's body.

Most fully licensed drivers must have a BAC below 0.05.

This also applies to most people supervising learner drivers.

Police can check a driver's BAC during roadside alcohol and drug testing.

These laws apply on both public roads and private property.

## Drivers who must have zero BAC

A driver must have a BAC of 0.00 if they:
- hold a learner permit
- hold a probationary licence, including P1 or P2
- have a driver's licence or learner permit with a Z or I condition
- are a restricted motorcycle rider with an E condition
- are a driving instructor
- drive a bus or commercial passenger vehicle, such as a taxi
- drive a rigid or articulated truck greater than 4.5 tonnes gross vehicle mass

For these drivers, driving with a BAC above 0.00 is an offence.

## Alcohol testing

Victoria Police conduct random roadside breath testing.

It is an offence to:
- fail to stop at a booze bus or random breath-testing station
- refuse to provide a breath or blood sample
- fail to cooperate with police conducting a breath or blood test

Refusing a breath test can result in serious penalties.

## Drinking alcohol in a vehicle

A person must not drink alcohol while:
- driving a motor vehicle
- sitting beside and supervising a learner driver

This applies even if the driver's or supervisor's BAC remains below their legal limit.

Passengers may drink alcohol in a vehicle, although doing so may distract the driver.

## Alcohol and driving ability

Alcohol can reduce a driver's ability to:
- concentrate
- make decisions
- react quickly
- judge speed and distance
- perform multiple tasks
- maintain coordination and balance

Alcohol can also cause drowsiness and increase risky behaviour.

A BAC of 0.05 is associated with approximately double the crash risk compared with a BAC of zero.

## Alcohol remaining in the body

Alcohol may remain in the body the following day.

Cold showers, exercise, coffee, fresh air or vomiting do not reduce the amount of alcohol in the bloodstream.

Drivers, especially those who must maintain a zero BAC, should consider whether alcohol consumed the previous night may still affect their ability or legal eligibility to drive.

## Mixing alcohol with drugs and medicines

Alcohol should not be combined with drugs or medicines when driving.

This includes:
- prescription medicines
- medicines bought from a pharmacy or supermarket
- illicit drugs

Combining alcohol with other substances can further reduce a person's ability to drive safely.

## Relevant legislation

Victorian laws concerning driving with alcohol and drugs are contained in Part 5 of the Road Safety Act 1986.
