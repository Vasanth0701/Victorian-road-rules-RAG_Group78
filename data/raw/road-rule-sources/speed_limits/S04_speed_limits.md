# Speed limits

Source ID: S04
Organisation: Transport Victoria
Source title: Speed limits
URL: https://transport.vic.gov.au/road-and-active-transport/road-rules-and-safety/speed-limits

## General speed limit rule

Drivers and riders must follow the speed limit shown on signs.

If there is no speed limit sign, the applicable default speed limit must be followed.

Drivers must travel at or below the speed limit and should also drive to the road and traffic conditions.

## Typical speed zones in Victoria

Different speed limits may apply depending on the road environment.

Examples include:

- 10 km/h in some areas where pedestrians have priority
- 20 km/h in some car parks and recreational areas where vehicles and pedestrians mix
- 30 km/h in some areas with high pedestrian, cycling or other active-transport activity
- 40 km/h in activity centres, some school zones and local streets
- 50 km/h on many urban roads and unsigned local streets in built-up areas
- 60 km/h on some higher-movement urban roads
- 70 km/h on some divided urban arterial roads and semi-regional urban roads
- 80 km/h on some high-movement roads and rural roads
- 100 km/h on many highways and rural roads
- 110 km/h on some freeways, motorways and tollways in non-built-up areas

These are examples only. Drivers must follow the speed limit that applies to the specific road.

## Default speed limits when no sign is displayed

Where there is no speed limit sign, a default speed limit applies.

### Built-up areas and neighbourhoods

The default speed limit is 50 km/h.

This applies to roads in suburban or built-up areas where no speed limit sign is displayed.

### Country roads

The default speed limit is 100 km/h.

This applies to roads outside built-up areas where no speed limit sign is displayed.

## Speed limit signs

Drivers must travel at or below the speed shown on the applicable speed limit sign.

Different types of speed limit signs may be used, including:
- standard speed limit signs
- time-based signs
- variable electronic signs
- temporary speed limit signs

## Roadworks

Temporary speed limits may apply around roadworks.

When approaching a roadworks speed sign, a driver must:
- slow down to the speed shown on the temporary sign
- travel at or below that limit
- continue following that limit until passing a sign showing a different speed limit

The temporary speed limit still applies even when roadworks or workers are not visibly present.

## Emergency, law enforcement and incident response scenes

Drivers must slow down when passing certain stationary or slow-moving emergency, incident-response or roadside-assistance vehicles displaying flashing lights or sounding an alarm.

Drivers must:
- safely slow to 40 km/h
- pass the scene at 40 km/h and be able to stop if directed
- return to the normal legal speed limit after reaching a safe distance from the scene

## Road Safety Road Rules 2017

Victorian speed-limit rules are contained in Part 3 of the Road Safety Road Rules 2017.

Relevant rules include:
- Rule 20 — Obeying the speed-limit
- Rule 21 — Speed-limit where a speed-limit sign applies
- Rule 22 — Speed-limit in a speed-limited area
- Rule 24 — Speed-limit in a shared zone
- Rule 25 — Speed-limit elsewhere

## Speeding penalties

Drivers and riders may receive fines and demerit points for exceeding the speed limit.

More serious speeding offences may result in additional penalties such as licence suspension or cancellation and vehicle impoundment or immobilisation.
